# Sources

Depths, landmarks and creature facts used in Ocean Dive v2. Where a range is marked *approx.* on the site, no primary source was confirmed and the figure is a commonly cited approximation.

## Depth model

- Challenger Deep 10,935 m (35,876 ft); average ocean depth 3,682 m: NOAA Ocean Exploration, https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/facts/ocean-depth.html
- Zones (sunlight 0–200 m, twilight 200–1,000 m, midnight 1,000–4,000 m, abyss 4,000–6,000 m, hadal 6,000 m+): NOAA JetStream, https://www.noaa.gov/jetstream/ocean/layers-of-ocean ; https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/light_travel.html
- Pressure: +1 atm per 10.06 m (33 ft) of seawater, NOAA Ocean Service, https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/pressure.html (site adds 1 atm of air; estimate).
- Temperature: illustrative typical open-ocean profile (≈20 °C surface, ≈4 °C at 1,000 m, 1–3 °C in the deep); varies by region. NOAA Ocean Service, https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/coldocean.html

## Landmarks

- 40 m recreational scuba limit: https://blog.padi.com/how-deep-can-open-water-vs-advanced-divers-go/
- 214 m deepest freedive (Nitsch, 2007): https://www.aidainternational.org/WorldRecords/History/NoLimit
- 332.35 m deepest scuba dive (Gabr, 2014): https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/deepest-scuba-dive-male
- 610 m sperm whale routine dive: https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/sperm-whale
- 2,086 m Everest summit marker = 10,935 − 8,849 m (Everest height, 2020 China–Nepal survey)
- 2,992 m Cuvier's beaked whale dive: Schorr et al. 2014, https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0092633
- 3,800 m (12,467 ft) RMS Titanic: NOAA, https://sanctuaries.noaa.gov/maritime/titanic/2010_expedition.html ; https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/explorations/03titanic/
- 8,336 m deepest fish filmed: Guinness, https://guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/deepest-fish ; Jamieson et al. 2023, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dsr.2023.104132
- 10,916 m Trieste (1960): commonly reported depth of the first descent.
